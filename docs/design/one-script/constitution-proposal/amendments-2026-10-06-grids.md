## 1. Addition to Article IV, beside "Ground ages" — A Ground that moves

> **Ground moves.** A recorded Ground SHALL carry a fingerprint of the passage
> it cites. Re-locating the Ground compares the fingerprint. Where the passage
> has changed or can no longer be found, the Ground is reported as **Cited
> (moved)**, saying which. Like staleness, a move is a report, not a demotion:
> it changes no tier and triggers no quorum. It states that what was cited is
> no longer what is there, which is a different claim from "no one has looked
> recently" and from "the evidence failed."

**Decision class:** Re-locating a Ground and reporting a move — Auto-Applied +
Ledger. The existing *Recording a claim's Ground* row would carry it.

**Why.** "Ground ages" (Draft 0.9) covers *time*: a Ground nobody has
re-located within the window is reported stale. Nothing covers *change*: a
Ground that was re-located and no longer says what was cited. The Definitions
already say a Ground "is a claim about where to look, never a report that
anyone has looked". A move is the report of someone looking and finding it
different, and the law has no word for it.

The evidence is from this session's
[branch table](../../branches/branches-table-2026-10-06.md). Each box in the
grids carries a fingerprint of the passage it copies. Merging either of two
open branches into the grids' branch, in a scratch copy, left the main table's
largest box (G5) and its six clauses (R1–R6) citing a passage that no longer
exists. Both branches rewrite the rule those boxes copy into a single line.
Nothing failed and nothing went stale. The cited text had simply moved, and
only the fingerprint could tell. Without this clause, that state is reported as
either "fine" or "broken", and it is neither.

**What it does not do.** It does not say which fingerprint, or how often to
re-locate. Those belong to the machinery, which cites this clause.
"Moved" and "stale" are independent: a Ground can be both, and both are
reported.

**Interested:** no. It binds the record, whoever cites.

## 2. Addition to the Definitions — One name, one referent

> **One name, one referent.** Within the record, a name (a term, an address,
> an identifier) refers to one thing. Where a name already in use is given to
> a second thing, the later use SHALL be qualified or renamed before it is
> recorded. A token that only resembles a name is set aside, and never linked
> or counted as a reference to it. An existing collision, once found, is
> recorded as a collision and resolved by qualification, never by silently
> choosing one referent.

**Decision class:** Recording a name collision — Auto-Applied + Ledger.
Renaming a term defined in this constitution — Amendment (Article VIII).

**Why.** The law has met this problem three times and solved it three
separate ways. Draft 0.7 carried three notes, each written for one collision:
"Independent Witness" against the reaction engine's "independent source";
three unrelated senses of "tier"; and two different files sharing one
filename ([`CONSTITUTION.proposed.md`](CONSTITUTION.proposed.md), the notes
marked box-scan A10). Draft 0.9 does not carry them. They named artifacts,
which the one-direction rule now forbids, and nothing general replaced them.
The collisions are still there. Only the rule for them is gone.

This session met it twice more:
- **Addresses.** The second grid first reused rows A–D, so "A1" named two
  different boxes. It was renumbered N–Q, and the rule was made mechanical: a
  new grid's rows start where the last one stopped, and two grids sharing a
  row letter are refused
  ([`../../one-box/boxes-outside-2026-10-06.md`](../../one-box/boxes-outside-2026-10-06.md)).
- **Look-alikes.** Linking the Gerald atoms to the main table, four tokens
  looked like box addresses and named something else: D10 and D7 (decisions),
  C3 (an invariant) and B1 (a boot step). They were set aside, not linked
  ([`../../gerald/gerald-to-main-2026-10-06.md`](../../gerald/gerald-to-main-2026-10-06.md)).
  "D7" is itself a live collision: the premise document's decision D7 and the
  one-box plan's decision D7 are different decisions.

One general clause replaces the three notes and covers what comes next.

**What it does not do.** It does not rename anything already recorded. An
existing collision is recorded and qualified from then on, and past entries
stay as they are (VI.2).

**Interested:** no. It binds names, whoever records them.
