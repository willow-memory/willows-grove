training and see what lands", then, offered to keep it as conversation: "All
data is data". This is the grid after the coverage layers (HG). Like grid two,

that looks like chance, and it says so here. The notes below are the agent's

references. Every box is `unattested`. The grid copies from these notes, the
same as it copies from any other source.*

## The choice

Eight pulls, sorted the way the agent ranked them in the conversation:

- **Row HH:** the ones that hit the work directly.
- **Row HI:** the ones that describe its method.
- **Row HJ:** mood.

Each note says what it is, then where it lands. The landing is the agent's
reading.

## The notes (the source)

### HH · Hit the work

- **The Library of Babel.** Borges (1941) imagines a library holding every possible book, nearly all of them gibberish. It lands on the coverage grid: 869 boxes, 99% full, and the law behind them mostly uncited. Full is not the same as meaningful.
- **Dewey 001.9.** The Dewey Decimal Classification gives "controversial knowledge" its own number instead of discarding it. It lands on the unknown-ID row: IDs that name no clause, kept and labelled rather than deleted.
- **The map is not the territory.** Korzybski's line (early 1930s): a representation is not the thing it represents. It lands on this round's fix. The coverage report read one spelling of a citation, so for the report the territory had one road.
- **The Voynich manuscript.** Every page is written and illustrated, and no one has read a word of it. It lands on the measure: text can be counted without anyone knowing what it says, and only a witness can say whether it is true.

### HI · Describe the method

- **Linnaeus's slips.** Late in his career Linnaeus kept his notes on small slips of paper of a standard size, an early form of the index card. It lands on the grid of addresses: a box can move, and its name stays put.
- **Mise en place.** A cook sets out every ingredient before the heat goes on. It lands on the ledger-first pattern: generate the source, then fill the grid. The Gerald craft notes say the same of writing: worldbuilding precedes drafting.

### HJ · Mood

- **Lisbon, 1755.** The earthquake struck on the morning of All Saints' Day, when many people were at Mass. Voltaire's poem on it argued against the comfort of "all is well". It lands on no smoothing: a disaster is not averaged into a fine day.
- **The rotisserie.** A spit turns so that the heat lands evenly. The canon says the goo drips "evenly, the just and the unjust", and the measure's Gini asks how evenly the text falls. The canon also says not to map the goo, so this one is mood and nothing more.
